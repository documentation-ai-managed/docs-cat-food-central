---
title: What is taurine and why does it matter for cats?
description: >-
  Taurine is an amino acid that is essential for cats - they cannot produce it
  themselves and must get it from their diet. Without it, cats develop serious
  and irreversible health problems.
---
<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">
  Taurine deficiency in cats causes dilated cardiomyopathy (an enlarged,
  weakened heart) and retinal degeneration leading to blindness. Both
  conditions can be irreversible if deficiency continues long enough.
</Callout>

## Why cats can't make their own taurine

Most mammals can synthesise taurine from other amino acids (cysteine and methionine). Cats have very limited ability to do this - their enzyme activity for taurine synthesis is far below what's needed to meet their requirements. They must get it pre-formed from food.

## Natural vs synthetic taurine

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="🥩 Natural taurine (from meat)" icon="star">
    Animal muscle meat - especially heart, dark meat poultry, and seafood -
    is naturally rich in taurine. A food with high-quality animal ingredients
    in sufficient quantity will provide taurine naturally.

    **Scoring:** Foods where taurine is provided naturally through quality animal ingredients earn bonus points under Rule 8.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="💊 Added taurine (synthetic supplement)" icon="plus">
    Most commercial cat foods add synthetic taurine as a supplement - listed
    as "taurine" in the ingredient list. This meets the cat's requirement and
    is perfectly safe, but it's often a sign that the base ingredients don't
    contain enough naturally occurring taurine on their own.

    **Scoring:** Added taurine doesn't earn the Rule 8 bonus, but it also
    doesn't trigger a deduction. It's neutral.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  Check for "taurine" in the ingredient list - its presence means it was
  added as a supplement. That's fine and better than nothing. But if you
  see high-quality animal ingredients like heart, dark poultry, or fish
  high up the list, natural taurine is likely well-covered.
</Callout>

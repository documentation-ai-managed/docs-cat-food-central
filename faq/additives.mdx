---
title: What about artificial colors and chemical additives?
description: >-
  Artificial colors, synthetic preservatives, and chemical flavor enhancers
  serve no nutritional purpose for cats and some have raised health concerns.
  The scoring system penalizes their presence.
---
<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Cats cannot see the red and orange colors in their food - they're put there
  to appeal to *cat parents*, not cats. Artificial coloring is purely cosmetic
  and adds zero nutritional value.
</Callout>

## What gets penalized and why

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="🧪 Synthetic preservatives (BHA, BHT, ethoxyquin)" default-open="false">
    BHA and BHT are petroleum-derived antioxidants used to prevent fat from
    going rancid. Ethoxyquin was originally developed as a pesticide and
    rubber stabiliser. All three have raised health concerns in animal
    studies. Natural alternatives like mixed tocopherols (Vitamin E) and
    rosemary extract work just as well.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="🎨 Artificial colors (Red 40, Yellow 5, Blue 2, etc.)" default-open="false">
    Added purely for visual appeal to the cat parent. Cats are partly
    colorblind and don't respond to food color. Some artificial dyes
    have been linked to hypersensitivity reactions in animals.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="🧂 Artificial flavor enhancers" default-open="false">
    Chemical compounds are added to make lower-quality ingredients more palatable. A signal that the base ingredients aren't appealing enough on their own - which often indicates lower overall quality.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="✅ What's fine: natural preservation" default-open="false">
    Mixed tocopherols (Vitamin E), rosemary extract, and citric acid are
    natural preservatives that don't trigger Rule 5 deductions. They're
    effective and far better tolerated than synthetic alternatives.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  Look for "preserved with mixed tocopherols" on the label - that's a
  good sign. If you see BHA, BHT, or ethoxyquin, expect a Rule 5 deduction.
</Callout>

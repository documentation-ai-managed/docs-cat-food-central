---
title: What are chelated minerals and why do they get bonus points?
description: >-
  Chelated minerals are bound to amino acids for better absorption. They're more
  bioavailable for cats than standard inorganic mineral forms - meaning more of
  the mineral actually reaches the cat's cells.
---
<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  "Chelated" comes from the Greek word for claw - the mineral is essentially
  grabbed and held by an amino acid, making it easier for the digestive
  system to absorb and transport it.
</Callout>

## Chelated vs standard minerals

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="✅ Chelated minerals" icon="zap">
    Bound to an organic molecule (usually an amino acid) for easier
    absorption through the intestinal wall. The body recognises the
    amino acid carrier and brings the mineral along with it.

    **Examples on labels:**

    - Zinc proteinate

    - Iron amino acid chelate

    - Copper proteinate

    - Manganese proteinate

    - Zinc methionine complex
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="⚪ Standard inorganic minerals" icon="minus">
    Basic salt forms that depend on stomach acid to break them down before
    they can be absorbed. Absorption rates are generally lower and more
    variable.

    **Examples on labels:**

    - Zinc oxide

    - Ferrous sulfate

    - Copper sulfate

    - Zinc sulfate

    - Iron oxide
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Why it matters for scoring

Better mineral absorption means the cat actually benefits from the minerals in the food - not just what's listed on the label. It's a sign of investment in ingredient quality and formulation care.

Foods using chelated minerals earn bonus points under Rule 9. It's not the biggest scoring factor, but it's a meaningful signal about overall formulation quality.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  Scan the bottom of the ingredient list for mineral forms. Seeing "proteinate" or "amino acid chelate" after mineral names is a good sign. "Oxide" or "sulfate" forms are the basic, cheaper alternatives.
</Callout>
